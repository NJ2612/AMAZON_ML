## 2. The five insights that drive every downstream decision

1. **Script asymmetry is the #1 blocker of recall.** S1 is 100% romanized; **11–19% of
   S2/S3 names are native script** (Devanagari, Tamil, Kannada, Gujarati, Telugu) plus
   mixed script. Without offline transliteration those rows can *never* match S1 —
   the recall ceiling is capped at the blocking stage. Measured, not guessed:
   `ராஜ் இன்வெஸ்ட்மெண்ட்ஸ் எல்எல்பி` ↔ `Raj Investments LLP`.
2. **Transliteration is phonetic, not exact** → matching stays fuzzy. `unidecode` turns
   the Tamil above into `raaj innnvesttmenntts elelpi`, not `raj investments llp`.
   So even after cleaning, cross-script matching needs **character-n-gram / edit-distance**
   similarity, never exact-token equality.
3. **The generic-name trap is real and large.** In S1, single generic words repeat
   hundreds of times as *distinct* businesses: `meridian`×567, `summit`×464, `family`×444,
   `apex`×368 (France: `bordeauxclub`×516). ~41% of S1 rows share a normalized-name key.
   **Name alone must never merge** — address agreement is the disambiguator, and under
   F₀.₅ a false merge costs 2× a miss.
4. **Address is the confirming signal.** In every true-match example, the physical address
   agrees (same street number + city) despite heavy noise; generic-name collisions sit at
   *different* addresses. Blocking on name, confirming on address is the precision lever.
5. **Everything must stay country/script-agnostic.** France (SARL/SAS/SASU, Rue/Boulevard)
   appears only at test time. No hard-coding to {US, India}; every test S1 row must appear
   in the output, singletons included.

## 3. Noise catalogue (from measured matched pairs)

**Names:** OCR/typos (`Wilblims`, `Enterpires`, `Sttfrifng`, `Etrepndiels`); Latin
diacritics (`Énterprises`, `Bóral`); legal-suffix drop / add / misplacement
(`LLC Crystal Staffing`, `[Corp] Dick Regional`, `Obsidian, [[LLC]]`, `L.L.P.`);
injected generic words (`… Center`, `… Service`, `… Partners`, `… Company`); dropped
core words (`Crystal` alone); website-as-name (`heassociates.com`); DBA
(`Korbrixx D.B.A. Obsidian`); junk prefixes (`--`, `<<`, `>>`, `#`, `[[ ]]`); injected phone numbers.

**Addresses:** component reordering (`KANSAS CITY, MO, 630 45ND TERRACE, null`); literal
`null` / `<NULL>`; state as code↔full↔native (`MO`↔`Missouri`, `TN`↔`Tamil Nadu`↔`தமிழ்நாடு`);
street abbrevs (`AVE`↔`Avenue`, `RD`↔`Road`, `ST`↔`Street`, even OCR `SAINT` for `St`);
number variants (`45th`↔`45ND`, `AF-684`↔`AF-0684`, `1056`↔`1056c`); ALL-CAPS in S2/S3;
landmarks (`Near Fortis Hospital`, `Opp.Rta Office`); PO boxes; ~3% empty.

## 4. Implication for preprocessing (what the cleaner must do)

- **Transliterate** any non-ASCII name/address to Latin (unidecode) — highest-impact step.
- **Normalize** case, punctuation/junk, `&`→`and`, strip website suffixes & literal `null`.
- **Legal suffixes:** extract → canonical token, and produce a suffix-free `name_core`.
- **Addresses:** expand street abbrevs, canonicalize state code↔full, drop landmarks/`null`,
  keep tokens (do **not** reorder — downstream uses token-set overlap).
- **Never destroy raw** — keep original columns; add normalized ones alongside.
